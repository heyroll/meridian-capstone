## Key facts from the report

### Consumer behavior: dining out vs. grocery

- The spending gap between dining out and grocery shopping totaled over
  $21 billion (Restaurant & Bars > Grocery Stores) by the end of 2024 —
  more than six times the $3.4 billion gap in January 2022. Dining-out
  spending rose 4.4% in 2024; grocery spending rose only 1.8%.
- Grocery store foot traffic reached nearly 17.2 billion visits in 2024
  (Placer.ai data) — up 1.0% from 2023 and up 10.9% from 2019. Average
  in-store dwell time fell to 23.4 minutes (down 0.3 min from 2023, down
  1.4 min from 2019), attributed partly to e-commerce substitution.
- **Discount and specialty grocers are outperforming on foot traffic:**
  Aldi's 2024 foot traffic rose to over 900 million visits (up 51.2%
  from 2019, from a ~600 million 2019–2021 average); Grocery Outlet rose
  to nearly 130 million (up 48.7% from 2019, from a ~87 million
  2019–2021 average); Trader Joe's rose to nearly 390 million (up 6.1%
  from 2023 and 24.7% from 2019).

### Grocery-anchored real estate fundamentals

- Grocery-anchored retail vacancy hit a new low of 3.5% as of Q4 2024 —
  10 basis points below Q4 2023, and 100 basis points below the
  pandemic-era peak of 4.5% (Q2 2021).
- Net absorption (real estate demand) totaled nearly 300,000 sq ft in
  2024 (down 37.9% from 2023); net deliveries (real estate supply)
  stayed under 100,000 sq ft for the second year running. Both figures
  are far below the 2015–2019 average (2.1M sq ft
  absorption, 1.1M sq ft deliveries per year) — new supply has been
  minimal since the pandemic.
- Available grocery-anchored retail space has shrunk by 7.5 million sq
  ft since its Q1 2021 pandemic-era peak, and by more than 19 million sq
  ft (nearly half) since its post-Great-Recession peak.
- Grocery-anchored rents rose 3.1% year-over-year in Q4 2024 — the
  highest growth rate of any retail subtype (vs. 2.9% for neighborhood
  and power centers).
- Average price per sq ft sold hit a record $209 in 2024.

### Investment activity

- Multi-tenant grocery-anchored retail transactions totaled $7.0 billion
  in 2024 (+1.4% from 2023), with a particularly strong second half
  (+34%) as benchmark interest rates fell 100 basis points.
- Private capital's share of investment volume fell from 74% (2023) to
  68% (2024) as REITs (Brixmor, Phillips Edison & Co, Cohen & Steers,
  ShopOne, Agree Realty, Regency Centers), while grocery operators
  (Publix, Trader Joe's, Walmart, H Mart, Weis Markets) instead became
  more active — operator investment volume hit an eight-year high.

### Grocer expansion in 2024 — who opened where

- **Aldi** remained the fastest-growing U.S. grocer: 105 new stores,
  2.3M+ sq ft added (a 6.7% decrease from 2023's record pace). Plans
  225+ new stores in 2025 and 800 by 2028 (organic growth plus
  conversion of 220 acquired Winn-Dixie/Harveys stores).
- **Publix** added 2M+ sq ft via 43 new stores, over half in Florida;
  entered its eighth Southeastern state (Kentucky) in early 2024.
- **H-E-B** added 1M+ sq ft via 9 new stores, all in Texas.
- **Sprouts** — the specialty grocer already tracked elsewhere in this
  wiki for its Pasadena/Highland Park expansion — opened 35 new stores
  nationally, adding just under 840,000 sq ft, with new-store openings
  up 16.7% from 2023. It entered Wyoming for the first time and grew
  its presence in Delaware, South Carolina, and Virginia, "using its
  cost-effective (smaller) store model."
- **Trader Joe's** added 450,000+ sq ft via 34 new stores.
- **The Fresh Market** added 8 new stores, just under 200,000 sq ft.
- **Amazon Fresh** "restarted" expansion (per the report's own framing)
  with 21 new stores, under 900,000 sq ft added — alongside continued
  Whole Foods growth. (This restart is nationwide; it does not
  contradict Amazon's separate 2026 California Fresh/Go closures
  covered elsewhere in this wiki, which post-date this report.)
- **Grocery Outlet** grew primarily via M&A (see below) rather than
  organic openings, entering Delaware and expanding in Ohio/Maryland.

### Mergers & acquisitions in 2024

- **Kroger–Albertsons:** the proposed merger was **blocked by the FTC**
  in December 2024 — it would have united the two chains and
  significantly increased their combined market share.
- **Aldi–Southeastern Grocers** (completed March 2024): enables Aldi's
  800-store 2028 plan via conversion of 220 acquired Winn-Dixie/Harveys
  Supermarkets stores.
- **Grocery Outlet–United Grocery Outlet** (completed Feb. 2024): 40
  stores acquired for Southeast expansion.
- **SpartanNash–Fresh Encounter Inc.** (completed Oct. 2024): 49
  supermarkets acquired across Kentucky, Indiana, and Ohio.
- **Jim Pattison Group (Save Mart)–[West Coast regional grocer]** (June
  2024): Canadian conglomerate expanded its U.S. presence via
  acquisition of a 190+ store West Coast regional grocer.
- **Hy-Vee–Strack & Van Til** (completed April 2024): 22-store chain
  acquired, giving Hy-Vee its ninth state (Indiana).

### Private label

- Private-label sales topped $270 billion in 2024 (+3.4% from 2023, per
  the Private Label Manufacturer Association (PLMA)), up $51.7 billion
  (23.6%) over the past four years. Market
  share reached 20.7%, up 160 basis points from 2021.
- Kroger's "Our Brands" accounted for $31B+ of its $150B FY2023 sales;
  Albertsons' "Our Brands" accounted for $16.5B+ of its $79.2B FY2023
  sales.
- New 2024 private-label launches included Grocery Outlet's "Go
  Brands," Sprouts' personal-care line "Real Root" (October),
  Albertsons' wine line "Bee Lightly" and treats/gifts/décor line
  "Overjoyed" (September), Amazon's sub-$5 grocery-essentials line
  "Amazon Saver" (with a 10% Prime discount), Giant Food Holdings'
  grocery-basics line "New Coast Foraging Company" (February), and
  SpartanNash's "Finest Reserve" line of pizzas, pastas, spices & wine
  (February).

### SNAP and e-commerce

- SNAP participation remained over 41.7 million people (over 12% of the
  U.S. population) in FY 2024 — up 16.8% from FY 2019. The average
  monthly benefit was $187, up 44.3% from FY 2019 (largely due to
  pandemic-era emergency allotments, which ended February 2023).
- Grocery e-commerce sales reached nearly $204 billion in 2024 (+10.7%
  from 2023, per eMarketer) — the first time surpassing $200B. Forecast
  to reach $270 billion by 2028 (7.4% CAGR).
- Retail media ad spending reached nearly $52 billion in 2024 (+20.4%
  from 2023, per eMarketer), forecast to nearly double to $100 billion
  by 2028 (17.1% CAGR). National grocers (Albertsons, Kroger) lead;
  regional/local grocers are following.
